# Penguin visual strategy

Build one convincing emperor penguin inside the actual runner, then expand. Use **Astra-led Blender authoring through Python**, in-house, for the mesh, silhouette, skinning and animation. Keep integration, materials, motion checks and performance verification in this project. First milestone is an approved unrigged shape from the rear gameplay camera at phone size, with a larger rear detail view of that same model.

The owner chose in-house Astra work and rejected the external artist proposal. Shape and rear surface work progressed into an authorized motion pass on October 12. Current work is the separate `penguin_motion_v1` candidate: waddle, jump and slide on a fresh Blender rig, reviewed through the actual Godot runner mesh/material path. Human motion approval and production promotion remain pending. Front/podium appearance remains a later review gate.

Use the corrected October 11 ImageGen reference set as the current working art direction: [stronger-lean rear waddle poses](../../art_src/characters/reference_boards/penguin-gameplay-v2-20261011/waddle-rear-strong-lean-sequence.png) and the [rear movement reference page](../../art_src/characters/reference_boards/penguin-gameplay-v2-20261011/index.html), with waddle, jump and belly-slide pose animatics. Earlier corrections remain in force: **waddle, not running**, and **more visible leaning during the waddle**. All active references show the penguin facing away; no front artwork or capture is part of this turn. The working character has a rounded charcoal crown/back, compact pear-shaped body, restrained golden neck patches, tapered flippers, short feather tail and orange webbed feet. Resolve small generative inconsistencies into one coherent 3D model. Previous multi-angle boards remain historical assets, not approval for a future front design. The first v2 waddle remains preserved as the earlier, insufficient-lean study.

Jump includes anticipation, lift, airborne shape and landing. The latest motion direction adds relaxed feet hanging beneath the body, small flipper waves and a slight forward body tilt while airborne. Belly slide keeps the chest/belly down and head facing away, with the trailing feet moving gently up and down and the flippers making small balancing movements. Previous rear jump/slide images remain appearance and framing references; the written motion direction below takes precedence where their poses or movement differ. ## Motion intent to support during rig design

Record motion intent now so the new mesh and rig can support it. Prove rough poses and short motion tests while fitting the rig; finish exact timing, amplitude, easing and secondary overlap after the shape and deformation are sound. These gameplay checks use the fixed rear camera. A later front/podium gate must also establish a credible presentation pose without distorting the face, chest, flippers or foot attachment.

| Action | Required motion intent | Early rig proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Waddle | Strong side-to-side weight transfer over alternating planted feet, compact steps and balancing flippers. | Body lean and each foot can be controlled independently; supporting sole stays grounded without dragging the other foot through the floor. |
| Belly slide | Chest/belly stays low against the course; trailing feet lift and lower gently; flippers move a little for balance. | Feet can move independently of torso pitch, and flippers remain free to move while the body is prone. Movement does not lift the entire body off its sliding contact. |
| Jump | Feet hang relaxed beneath the body rather than remaining rigidly tucked; flippers wave a little; body tilts forward slightly in the air. | Hanging feet remain readable behind the torso, both flippers can make small waves, and torso pitch can change without locking the feet/flippers into that same rotation. |

These are owner-requested movement characteristics, not fixed numeric animation curves. Do not invent a required kick count, synchronized/alternating foot pattern, wingbeat rate or pitch angle at this stage. Use restrained movement readable at phone size, and tune phase relationships during the rough motion proof. Preserve controlled takeoff, landing and slide transitions; visual body tilt and secondary limb motion do not change the gameplay jump trajectory, collision or travel authority. The earlier eight-pose GIFs do not yet demonstrate these added details, and no new animation implementation is claimed.

## In-house Blender workflow

Blender holds the editable **mesh, armature, skin weights and named animation actions** in one `.blend` source. Astra writes and iterates the Blender Python authoring code. A skinned, animated `.glb` exports that package for Godot; the existing `HeroPenguinRig` and `PenguinVisual` keep gameplay control. Exporting does not create a separate rig after modeling: the model is rigged and animated in Blender before export.

Default to a **new skeleton fitted to the design**. The [canonical source](../../art_src/characters/hero_penguin_v1.blend) and [existing builder](../../tools/art/build_hero_penguin_v1.py) are historical implementation references, not required bone, rest-pose or pivot templates. The current 15-joint rig parents each foot directly to the pelvis, with no thigh/shin/knee chain. Its waddle authoring goes further: feet/toes must retain identity local transforms while the pelvis rocks, and validation rejects independent foot animation. These choices constrain grounded stepping and should not dictate the new character.

First approve the unrigged rear silhouette and required gameplay poses, then resolve the front/podium design before committing rest pose or skin weights. Do not construct rear-only anatomy that prevents a credible front presentation. Place pelvis/body pivots, foot controls and any hidden leg or flipper chains around the approved shape. Author body roll and foot planting independently; bake the resulting transforms to the deformation bones exported in GLB. New skin weights and deformation topology must fit that skeleton. Bone names, hierarchy, rest transforms and pivots can change. Replace the legacy "feet never move locally" and unchanged-rest-rig checks with tests of support contact, penetration, unintended foot sliding, silhouette/deformation quality and root-motion ownership.

Preserve the **external gameplay interface** through a small adapter: pose/event requests, cadence, render role and named attachment sockets. `run` can remain the logical name for waddle; `head_socket`, `face_socket`, `neck_socket` and `trail_socket` remain the public attachment names, with placements fitted to the new model. Godot's controller discovers animation players and sockets rather than requiring this exact 15-bone layout. Gameplay continues to own world travel, jump displacement and lane banking. Existing animation curves are not assumed compatible with the new rest pose: waddle/jump/slide and all remaining required states must be authored or retargeted and verified before promotion. Current full coverage is 23 clips; an isolated prototype must declare incomplete states rather than imply that copied names establish working animation.

Deliverables are the reproducible Python builder/configuration, editable `.blend`, exported `.glb`, adapter/material inputs and rear waddle/jump/belly-slide runtime proof. The owner's current order is rear shape, rear surface detail, front/podium design, then rig/motion and final integration. The source tooling and [deterministic exporter](../../tools/art/blender_export.py) currently pin Blender **5.2.0**; verify the available executable before implementation. Export baked deformation bones, skins, named actions, UVs, normals/tangents and required morphs with explicit axis/scale conversion and the four-influence limit. The [export runner](../../tools/art/export_art.py) is reusable infrastructure; a new hero still needs explicit export registration/material binding. Rebuild any LOD skin bindings and attachment mappings for the new skeleton. Keep candidate output isolated until approved.

Use the current budget as the initial contract: at most 25,000 LOD0 triangles, 8,000–12,000 LOD1 triangles for the finished asset, at most two surfaces, 36 deform bones and four skin influences. Keep root-motion displacement disabled; gameplay owns movement. LOD1, full motion polish, species and cosmetics are completion requirements, not prerequisites for the first shape sketch. Preserve current validation minimums or revise the contract explicitly if a lower-poly final asset is justified. Triangle counts alone do not prove FPS. See [character contract](../../scripts/art/character_look.gd) and [candidate budgets](../../tools/art/build_hero_penguin_v12_candidate.py).

## Preserve the look through import

Keep one authoritative geometry/material package for the hero. The same intended runtime shader and textures must be used in the character viewer and runner; use scene lighting to explain remaining differences. Do not feed literal vertex colors or a unique baked atlas into the legacy shader's semantic-mask format.

Carry forward the existing [atlas contract](../../art_src/characters/hero_penguin_atlas_contract.md) principles: hashes binding mesh and maps, explicit color-space handling, and checked UVs/normals. This isolated surface candidate uses standard glTF embedded base-color, normal and metallic/roughness PNG maps, with strict embedded-byte and imported-pixel verification; no legacy material substitution. Preserve the prior literal COLOR_0 validator separately. Bake from the accepted new mesh and source; older successful maps belong to their original geometry. Godot exposes import and material overrides, and warns that procedural Blender materials may not transfer correctly, so the final appearance must be checked after import. [Godot import configuration](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/assets_pipeline/importing_3d_scenes/import_configuration.html).

Use a simple material for early shape review, then the intended production material for fidelity acceptance. Keep diagnostic unlit/normal views separate from artistic approval. Headless tests can prove structure and motion math; they cannot approve lit appearance.

Later, make character previews and thumbnails from the accepted runtime character where appropriate. Existing illustrated wardrobe art remains unchanged during the pilot. A broader wardrobe change is a separate product decision; its current illustrations cannot serve as proof of avatar fidelity.
